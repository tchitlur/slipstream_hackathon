# Slipstream: project summary

**Team tnt (Tanay Chitlur, solo). Hack-Nation Challenge 05, "AI Atlas for the World's Rare Diseases". Live at https://slipstreamhackathon.vercel.app/**

**The problem.** A parent who leads a small rare-disease group has three questions nobody answers in one place: who shares our disease, what already exists, and what should we do next. Most of the 7,000 rare diseases have no registry, no natural history study and no trial, while a neighboring community two genes away may have all three. Finding that neighbor, and knowing what can safely be borrowed from them, takes months of expert time that small groups do not have.

**What we built.** Slipstream is a readiness atlas for monogenic epilepsies and related neurodevelopmental disorders. Type a gene and it shows the "road" the condition is on (how the gene breaks), the communities ahead on the same road, and an eight-rung readiness ladder built from public records: gene-disease link, phenotype data, patient organization, registry, published mechanism, NIH funding, targeted trial, approved therapy. For any neighbor it says what can be borrowed (registry design, outcome measures, clinical network) and what must not be (a therapeutic strategy for the opposite mechanism), each verdict with its strongest counter-reason, and drafts a one-page letter to that community with every sentence footnoted to its source.

**Who benefits.** Patient-group leaders, clinicians advising them, and funders deciding where a small grant moves a community fastest.

**What works today.** 2,866 curated gene-disease records, 83 conditions with every layer, 212 classified trials, 413 NIH projects, 51 organizations and 6 shared registries; 19,992 transfer verdicts, each with a counter-reason; 1,230 quotes verified verbatim against their sources. Nothing is a black box: every cell opens its evidence, the four kinds of evidence never mix, and when the data supports no step, the product says so.
